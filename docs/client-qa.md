## ১ · সহজ কথায় ধারণা

**৩০ সেকেন্ডে:**
> ব্যস্ত ট্রেনের টিকিট এখন যে সবচেয়ে দ্রুত ক্লিক করে, সে পায়। তাই বট, দালাল আর বাড়তি অ্যাকাউন্টওয়ালারা এগিয়ে থাকে। আমাদের প্রস্তাব: ব্যস্ত ট্রেনে নির্দিষ্ট সময়ের মধ্যে সবাই আবেদন করবেন, তারপর প্রকাশ্য ও যাচাইযোগ্য ড্র হবে। প্রতিজন যাত্রী একটিই আবেদন করতে পারবেন, টিকিট থাকবে যাত্রীর নিজের নামে ও নিজের ফোনে, আর কেউ টিকিট হাতবদল করতে পারবে না। ফলে টিকিট কিনে বেশি দামে বিক্রি করা কঠিন হয়ে যায়।

**২ মিনিটে:**
১. **সমান সুযোগ:** ব্যস্ত ট্রেনে ০৮:০০–১০:০০-এর মধ্যে যেকোনো সময় আবেদন করলে সুযোগ একই। দ্রুত ইন্টারনেট বা বটে কোনো লাভ নেই।
২. **প্রকাশ্য ড্র:** তালিকা আগেই স্থির হয়, ড্র হয় প্রকাশিত দৈব সংখ্যা দিয়ে। যে কেউ পরে মিলিয়ে দেখতে পারেন।
৩. **এক যাত্রী, এক আবেদন:** অ্যাপ, ওয়েবসাইট, কাউন্টার বা এজেন্ট — যেখান থেকেই আবেদন হোক, একজন যাত্রীর একটিই আবেদন থাকে।
৪. **টিকিট যাত্রীর:** সাহায্যকারী বা এজেন্ট টিকিট নিজের করে নিতে পারেন না। নাম বদলানো বা হাতবদলের সুযোগ নেই।
৫. **গেটে ও ট্রেনে যাচাই:** কর্মী টিকিট আর মানুষটিকে আলাদাভাবে মিলিয়ে দেখেন, আর প্রতিটি যাচাই রেকর্ড হয়।
৬. **স্বচ্ছ দাম ও অভিযোগ:** খাতওয়ারি অফিসিয়াল মোট দেখানো হয়, SMS-এ রসিদ যায়। বাড়তি টাকা চাইলে অ্যাপ, SMS (EXTRA) বা কাউন্টারে গোপনে জানানো যায়।

## ৫ · Likely questions and answers (presenter prep)

### Concept and scope
**Q1. What is this, in one line?**
A fairer way to sell and check tickets on busy trains. Everyone applies in a window, a public draw decides, and each ticket stays with the named traveller.

**Q2. Is this live, or is it the railway's system?**
No. It's a proposed concept with fictional data. It isn't connected to any real system and isn't affiliated with the railway.

**Q3. Does it replace the current system?**
No, it upgrades it. The booking and checking journeys can sit on top of the existing back end. Normal, non-busy trains can stay first come, first served.

**Q4. What exactly have you built?**
- **Figma:**
  - the full journeys in English and Bangla;
  - mobile and desktop screens;
  - staff, desk, reviewer and operations screens;
  - an SMS demo;
  - a 79-screen connected prototype on page 99;
  - role journeys on page 98.
- **Coded prototype (React):** 487 automated tests pass, and the accessibility check found 0 problems across 340 screen states.
- **Documents:** decisions, the rules and a simulation.

### Fairness and the draw
**Q5. Isn't a draw unfair to people who wake up early?**
Speed rewards bots and paid helpers far more than ordinary people. In the window, everyone who applies has the same chance. It's used only on trains with more requests than seats.

**Q6. How do we know the draw isn't rigged?**
- The applicant list and seat count are frozen and fingerprinted before the random number exists.
- The random number comes from a public source.
- An independent auditor replays the result.
- A public page shows the result without names, and anyone can check their own application number.
- Nobody can re-run a draw.

**Q7. What if I'm not picked?**
You can join the waiting list. Released seats are offered strictly in order, and you keep your place. You can also apply on another train.

**Q8. Isn't it slower than buying instantly?**
For busy trains the result comes at 10:30 and payment is due by 11:00. That is less stressful than racing at 8 am. Normal trains stay instant.

### Black marketing and identity
**Q9. How does this actually reduce black marketing?**
It removes the five tools resellers rely on:
1. speed and bots;
2. extra accounts;
3. seat parking;
4. changing the name on a ticket;
5. hidden fees.

Ticket checks at the gate and on the train make resold tickets risky to use.

**Q10. By how much will it fall?**
We can't promise a number yet. A pilot on a few busy routes would measure:
- resale listings;
- name mismatches at checks;
- extra-charge reports;
- unexplained seats.

**Q11. Can't people still use other real people's NIDs?**
Partly. Consent codes go to each traveller's own phone, tickets stay in the traveller's name, and patterns are reviewed. Coordinated real people are the hardest case, and we say so openly.

**Q12. How do groups travel together?**
- A group is one application with one place in the draw.
- It is split into linked payment packages, and each package pays its own share.
- A child always stays with an accompanying adult.

**Q13. What about people without a smartphone?**
- Station counters (help is free) and registered agents.
- SMS for help and reports.
- Paper tickets.
- The whole service in Bangla.

### Agents and helpers
**Q14. Will agents lose their business?**
Registered agents stay. Their fee is fixed and printed on the receipt. They get the same chance as the app, and they never control the ticket.

**Q15. Can an agent or helper still cheat the passenger?**
Every step is visible to the passenger:
- they must give the agent a consent code from their own phone;
- they get an SMS receipt;
- they can report an extra charge;
- they can remove a helper at any time.

A secret cash deal that both sides hide can't be seen.

### Staff and stations
**Q16. Won't ID checks slow boarding?**
Staff scan the ticket and glance at the ID. If they can't check, they record "couldn't check" and the passenger goes on. Random samples keep it honest without blocking queues.

**Q17. What if staff are dishonest?**
- Every check and override is recorded.
- Desk decisions need a second approver, and nobody can approve their own change.
- Operations reconcile every seat.
- An independent integrity team reviews samples.

Software can't fix collusion alone.

**Q18. What happens at stations with poor internet?**
Offline checks are marked provisional and sync later. Reissuing a ticket cancels the old QR, so a copied ticket shows up after sync.

### Money and reports
**Q19. Where do refunds go?**
Always to the account that paid. A helper or agent can't redirect them.

**Q20. What happens after an extra-charge report?**
- The report creates one case, whether it came by app, SMS or counter.
- An independent team checks it against receipts and other reports.
- The ticket stays valid, and there is no automatic penalty.
- The passenger is told the outcome and can appeal.

**Q21. Is the SMS service real?**
Not yet. It's a simulation, and it needs a two-way SMS number and telecom support. It never asks for a full NID, PIN, password or payment code.

### Data and privacy
**Q22. Is NID data safe?**
- Screens show masked numbers and "NID record matched" only.
- The public audit shows no names.
- Staff see only what they need.

Final legal and data-protection checks are still to be done with the railway.

### Implementation
**Q23. What do you need from the railway?**
- Approval of the proposed rules (#35–#44).
- NID linking.
- An independent review team.
- A two-way SMS number.
- Staff training.
- A pilot on a few busy routes.

**Q24. How long will it take, and what will it cost?**
Not estimated yet. That needs an engineering discovery with the railway's technical team. We recommend a small pilot first.

**Q25. How will we measure success?**
Against a baseline, before and after the pilot:
- resale listings;
- name mismatches;
- extra-charge reports;
- unexplained seats;
- complaints;
- time at the gate;
- passenger satisfaction.

**Q26. What are the risks?**
- People may not trust the draw at first. The public audit and clear messaging address this.
- Some agents may push back.
- Identity linking depends on other systems.
- Staff adoption takes training.

### Tips for presenting
- Say "proposed" and "pilot" often. Never promise a percentage drop.
- Demo order:
  1. page 99, act 1 (apply → draw → pay);
  2. the SMS EXTRA report;
  3. the guard's separate person check;
  4. the agent portal (consent code, fee on the receipt).
- If asked something outside the design (cost, law, telecom), say it needs a joint discovery with the railway.
